Section I

Executive Brief

What It Is

A multi-agent AI system that continuously screens ~500 equities, 11 sector ETFs, 20 country ETFs, and key commodities to generate hedged long/short trade recommendations. Twenty-one specialized agents — fundamental analysts, macro strategists, technical traders, a risk manager, and a portfolio manager — operate within a structured debate and approval pipeline. Every output is advisory; a human makes the final execution decision.

How It Works

Fundamental & Macro agents screen universe continuously ↓ Agent identifies opportunity → publishes thesis with conviction score ↓ Other agents debate (adversarial, evidence-based, 2-3 rounds) ↓ Technical agent scores the setup (entry, stops, levels) ↓ Risk agent gates: factor betas, concentration, drawdown budget ↓ PM agent sizes, sets parameters, delivers recommendation ↓ Human reviews and executes discretionarily

Key Design Principles

  1. Every bet is hedged — single names vs. equal-weight sector ETF, sector ETFs vs. RSP, country ETFs vs. DM/EM broad index
  2. Factor-neutral discipline — book never exceeds ±0.6 beta to DXY, SPX, rates, VIX, oil, credit, gold, growth/value, large/small
  3. Dynamic leverage — scales with P&L and correlation regime (higher when winning + uncorrelated)
  4. Advisory only — no auto-execution, preserves discretionary overlay
  5. Audit trail — every thesis, debate, score, and decision is logged and reviewable

Questions for Discussion

Is ±0.6 the right tolerance? Systematic funds often run ±0.3; discretionary macro may accept ±1.0 on conviction.

Should some factors have tighter limits than others? (e.g., SPX at ±0.4 if truly market-neutral, CL1 at ±0.8 if energy is a competency)

Section IV

Anticipated Questions & Prepared Answers

Organized by likely conversation flow — from "what is this" to skeptical pushback to logistics.

The Basics

"Walk me through what this actually does."

Twenty-one AI agents — each acting like a specialized analyst or PM — continuously screen our universe of S&P 500 names, sector ETFs, country ETFs, and commodities. When one finds an opportunity, it writes up a thesis and proposes a hedged trade. Other agents challenge the idea in a structured debate. If it survives, a technical agent scores the setup, a risk agent checks it against our limits, and a PM agent sizes it and sends us the recommendation. We review and decide whether to execute. Nothing auto-trades.

"So it's a quant screen with extra steps?"

No. A quant screen ranks on factors and spits out a list. This system reasons. Each agent builds a thesis — "XOM is cheap because the market is mispricing their Permian breakeven at $45 when it's actually $38, and the coming OPEC cut will tighten the S/D balance." Then another agent challenges that. The originator responds with evidence. That adversarial process is the difference — it's closer to an investment committee than a screen.

"Why not just use ChatGPT and ask it for trade ideas?"

Three reasons: (1) No memory — a chat forgets your book, limits, and prior trades. This system persists beliefs and evolves them. (2) No risk enforcement — a chat will happily suggest levered, correlated, unhedged positions. Our system structurally rejects anything breaching limits. (3) Scale — you can't run 21 simultaneous specialists debating each other in a chat window.

The Edge

"Where's the alpha?"

Not in the AI being smarter than a human on any single name. It's structural:

  1. Coverage breadth. 500 names + 6 macro regions screened continuously. No fatigue, no distraction.
  2. Process discipline. Every idea debated, scored, risk-checked, sized by formula. No style drift.
  3. Speed to conviction. Overnight OPEC cut at 2 AM → sized trade recommendation before the open.
"Aren't LLMs just pattern-matching? How do they generate real insight?"

The agents aren't in a vacuum — they're fed real-time data. The LLM provides the analytical framework; fresh data provides the edge. The debate protocol is the quality filter: if an agent hallucinates, another challenges it with real data. Bad ideas get killed. We trust the process, not any single output.

"What's the win rate going to be?"

Honest answer: we don't know yet. System is designed for 55-60% hit rate with asymmetric payoffs (5% target vs. 2-3% stop = ~2:1 R/R). At 55% win rate with 2:1 reward/risk, the math works to 12-15%. We track per-agent performance and dial down underperformers.

Skepticism & Pushback

"LLMs hallucinate. How do you trust them with trade decisions?"

We don't trust any single output. Four layers of defense:

  1. Structured validation — strict schema parsing rejects nonsensical outputs.
  2. Adversarial debate — other agents challenge with real data.
  3. Risk gate — quantitative checks (betas, concentration) don't require understanding the thesis.
  4. Human decision — nothing auto-executes. You read everything and decide.
"What happens when all 21 agents get the same thing wrong?"

Correlated error scenario. Three mitigants: (1) Agents are specialized differently — they use different data and frameworks. Correlated failure requires simultaneous failure on different axes. (2) Risk framework is model-agnostic — factor limits block overconcentration regardless of thesis quality. (3) Human is the final filter — if it feels wrong, don't trade it.

"This sounds expensive. ROI math?"

$750/month = $9K/year. On a $10M book targeting 12-15%, that's $1.2-1.5M target return. System needs to generate 0.09% alpha to break even. One good trade pays for a year. The real question is whether incremental coverage and discipline generate even marginal alpha above what you'd do alone.

"What if market regime changes?"

Agents aren't static models — they're reasoning engines fed live data. When regime shifts, the data shifts, and conclusions shift. Macro agents explicitly track regime and adjust frameworks. If prompts need updating (a new regime where old frameworks fail), that's a quarterly maintenance task, not a fundamental flaw.

"How is this different from what Graham already does?"

This isn't systematic in the traditional sense — no backtest, no optimized parameters, no signal decay. It's discretionary macro enhanced by AI. Key differences: human-in-the-loop, narrative theses, flexible holding periods. Think of it as a complement — it occupies the discretionary alpha space (variant perception, catalyst ID) that systematic can't reach, but with systematic-grade risk discipline.

Implementation & Logistics

"How long until live?"

POC (end-to-end on small subset): 4-6 weeks. Full production (21 agents): ~30 weeks. We start generating real recommendations from the POC subset (Energy + Commodities) within 2 months while building the rest.

"What do you need from me?"
  1. Risk parameter calibration — 30 minutes to align on limits, leverage, stops.
  2. Investment process validation — edge cases or rules not captured?
  3. Data source priorities — if one paid feed to start, which matters most?
  4. Ongoing feedback — which recommendations make sense vs. don't, so we tune agents.
"Where does this run? Compliance?"

Personal device at home, completely outside firm ecosystem. No firm data in; no firm systems touched. Access via encrypted private network (Tailscale). Output is trade ideas only — execution is manual in a personal account.

"What if you leave or this breaks?"

Everything documented. Standard open-source tools (Python, PostgreSQL, Redis). Moderate Python experience can maintain it. Degrades gracefully — if system goes down, positions have stops at broker level regardless.
